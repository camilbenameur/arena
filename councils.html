How the Trend council decides

  1. 1

    The engine decides. At each 4-hour bar close (00:00, 04:00, 08:00… UTC), it gives the target for each contract: long, short or flat, with a size scaled to volatility.

  2. 2

    The AIs can brake. Three Haiku (news, data, risk) advise, an Opus President sets a coefficient of 1, 0.5 or 0 per contract. It can neither increase, nor invert, nor open: without an opinion, the engine plays alone.

  3. 3

    The code executes the gap between target and position, only if it exceeds 5% of the portfolio, with a 5% stop. The guardrail can reject; the order is then reduced until it passes.

Council rules: leverage, stops, budget

The Trend council: method

The initial ambition was a council that generates net income “of the order of 10% per day”. Ten percent per day, compounded, is ×17 in a month: no known strategy sustains that pace. The page therefore shows the target and the realized return per day, side by side.

The former councils (Haiku and Opus) lost money: opinions without an edge, huge sizes, a new decision every 10 minutes. The Trend council reverses the roles: an engine decides, the AIs can only brake, the code executes. Paper demonstration, outside the retail rule (2x): could not go live as it is.

  • The engine: slow trend following (2-day and 6-day moving averages on 4-hour bars, long and short, size scaled to volatility, 2x scale), chosen among 146 trials from four strategy families and frozen before the holdout was read. Over 31 holdout days in a sharp decline it made +21% (drawdowns down to −25%); in a trendless market it hovers around zero. One strategy, one episode of each type: the edge is plausible, not proven.
  • The AIs: three Haiku (news, data, risk) advise, an Opus President sets for each contract a coefficient of 1, 0.5 or 0 that multiplies the engine's target. They can neither increase, nor invert, nor open a position the engine does not have. If the President fails or times out, the engine plays alone.
  • The pace: one round at each 4-hour bar close (00:00, 04:00… UTC), plus a veto round on a Kraken announcement, a macro release or a 2% move in an hour, which can only lower the coefficients. No call every 10 minutes, no recall requested by an AI.
  • The execution: an order only if the gap between target and position exceeds 5% of the portfolio; a 5% stop on each position. If the guardrail rejects an order for its size, the code retries it smaller.
  • The “engine alone” benchmark plays the same target, without AI, through the same path and on the same rounds: the gap with the Trend council measures what the AIs add or remove.
Leverage
At most 10, with Kraken's margins; the engine's scale gives about 1.4 on average, with peaks around 4 to 6. An order up to 400% of the portfolio's value.
Stop
5% on each position; loss at the stop of 25% of the portfolio at most per position, 59% for all stops combined.
Cost
The cost of a round trip does not exceed a third of the expected move. The engine's is measured offline (+2.47% gross per trade over the 64 training days), not declared.
Pace
At most 48 openings or adjustments per 24 h, with no delay between two; no daily freeze, no fee budget (fees remain real and counted).
Stop-out
